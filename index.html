Hospital Readmission Risk

Complete

A binary classification model that predicts 30-day hospital readmission for diabetic patients, trained on the UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset (~101,000 patient encounters, 50 features).

  • Engineered a modular preprocessing pipeline producing 80 model-ready features with zero null values
  • Trained a Random Forest classifier with balanced class weighting to address an 11% positive-class rate
  • Applied SHAP for global feature attribution and patient-level (local) explanations
  • Validated feature drivers against clinical domain knowledge and documented methodology and results in the project README
Result: ROC-AUC of 0.67 on a highly imbalanced clinical outcome, with global and local SHAP explanations that make each prediction auditable.

Loan Outcome Prediction

Complete

A binary classification study on 45,000 loan applications comparing an interpretable logistic regression with a random forest ensemble. Team project for STOR 320; I owned the full technical implementation, from data cleaning through modeling and evaluation.

  • Removed implausible outliers, log-transformed right-skewed income, and one-hot encoded categorical features
  • Built a stratified 80/20 train-test split and a 77.8% majority-class baseline
  • Evaluated both models on a shared holdout set using accuracy, ROC-AUC, precision, recall, F1, and confusion matrices
  • Documented limitations, including potential target leakage, class imbalance, and signs of a synthetic data source
Result: Random forest achieved 92.95% accuracy and 0.976 ROC-AUC versus 84.56% and 0.856 for logistic regression, raising minority-class recall from 0.49 to 0.77 (F1 0.59 to 0.83).

Securing the AI Frontier: U.S.–New Zealand AI Policy Alignment

In progress

A team NLP research project for DATA 520 that asks whether bilateral AI governance and security gaps between the U.S. and New Zealand cluster around specific policy domains. Findings are intended to inform U.S. State Department cooperation efforts.

  • Building a corpus of U.S. and New Zealand AI strategy, regulation, and governance documents from the OECD AI Policy Observatory, split into standardized text chunks to control for differences in document volume
  • Applying LDA topic modeling to identify recurring policy themes, then mapping topics to governance domains such as safety, security, and transparency
  • Selecting topic counts with coherence scores and seed-stability checks, and measuring inter-annotator agreement on topic labels with Cohen's kappa
  • Quantifying cross-country differences with Jensen-Shannon divergence and a permutation test, with robustness checks across chunk sizes and data sources
